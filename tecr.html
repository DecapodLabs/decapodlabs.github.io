The End of Continuous Rework.

November 19, 2026

We have Continuous Integration (CI) and Continuous Delivery (CD). Apparently, with AI, we've also invented Continuous Rework (CR). Agentic engineering often feels like a series of cognitive bubbles, squeezing out a generation for each thought, finding something worth keeping, and moving on. Except every iteration risks regressing on something we've already solved. So we keep reshaping the same lump of clay, hoping eventually it resembles what we had in mind.

When I started building Decapod in 2025, I wanted to eliminate this cycle. I was tired of constantly recontextualizing agents, correcting assumptions, explaining the same requirements, and watching perfectly good engineering decisions unravel between iterative generations. I don't want to have to explain the universe just to get an agent to understand the scope I already have in my head.

I want to be lazy with my prompt and still get my intended outcomes. I believe agents should carry the burden of resolving ambiguity, discovering context, exercising engineering judgment, and proving their work. The quality of an engineering outcome shouldn't depend on how exhaustively a human can articulate every requirement. That's the entire point of delegating work to something capable of reasoning.

So I'm giving Continuous Rework a name, and declaring that it has no place as the accepted standard for agentic engineering. The burden of translating human intent into verifiable engineering outcomes belongs to the agentic system. That's the conviction behind Decapod, and it's a standard my agents have operated against since February of this past winter (2026). I set out to eliminate Continuous Rework, and I intend to see it buried. Consider this its introduction and obituary, all in one.

Very Best,
Alex H. Raber
